# II. Behind the scenes

* Once one is selected for an interview, they are usuarll go through a screening interview
* In an on-site interview round, there are usuarlly 3 to 6 in-person interviews
* The distribution of questions varies between companies and even teams due to company priorities, size and just pure randomness

## The Microsoft Interview

* They want smart people, Geeks. People who are passionate about technology
* They tend to give teams more individual control, and the product set is diverse

## The Amazon Interview

* The "bar raiser" interviewer is charged with keeping the interview bar high. The person in charge of this has both significant experience with interviews and veto power in the hiring decision
* Amazon cares a lot about scale
* Amazon tends to ask a lot of questions about object-oriented design

## The Google Interview

* On the on-site interview, the candidate will interview with four to six people
* Feedback is typically broken down into four categories (Analytical Ability, Coding, Experience, and Communication) an the candidate is given an overall score from 1.0 to 4.0

## The Apple Interview

* Apple does two-on-one interviews often

## The Facebook Interview

* During the on-site interview, the candidate will interview primarily with other software engineers, but hiring managers are also involved whenever they are available
* Each interviewer is given a "role" during the on-site interviews, which helps that there are no repetitive questions and that they get a holistic picture of a candidate
    * Behavioral ("Jedi"): This interview assesses the candidate's ability to be successful in Facebook's environment
    * Coding and Algorithms ("Ninja"); These are the standard coding and algorithms questions
    * Design/Architecture ("Pirate"): For a backend software engineer, the candidate might be asked system design questions. Front-end or other specialties will be asked design questions related to that discipline


## The Palantir Interview

*  Many candidates report that Palantir's questions were harder than those they saw at Google and other top companies
